# Korean Bone Broth (Seolleongtang)

- A mix of beef bones - 
  - marrow bones (sagol)
  - knuckle bones (dogani)
  - feet (ujok)
- water to fill the pot
- 1-2 lbs of beef brisket or shank meat

A few pounds of bones go a long way, and you can use as much water as
your pot can hold.

1. Soak the bones in cold water for one hour or more to draw out
   the blood. Rinse well and drain.

2. Soak the meat in a separate bowl for one hour as well. Drain 
   and refirgerate until needed.

3. Add bones to a large stock pot (8qt or more) and cover with cold
   water. Bring to a boil over high heat. Reduce the heat to medium and
   boild for 5 minutes.

4. Drain. Rinse the bones, and clean out the pot to remove any brown
   bits. Return the bones to the pot.

5. Fill up the pot with cold water, leaving a little room for boiling.
   Bring it to a boil over high heat, and reduce the heat to medium.

6. Cover, and boil until the broth becomes rich and milky, about 5 hours
   (or longer if you have time). Adjust the heat a little, if necessary,
   to maintain a medium boil. Add more water (boiling hot water to
   maintain the cooking temperature) to cover the bones, once or twice
   while boiling.

7. Add the soaked meat (and more water if needed to submerge the meat).
   Boil until the meat is tender, for 1-1/2 to 2 hours. Remove the
   meat. Once cooled, thinly slice the meat to add to the soup when
   serving. Pour the broth through a colander into another pot or a
   large bowl to cool.

8. [Optional step for milkier broth] Fill up the pot with fresh water
   again. Bring to a boil over high heat. Reduce the heat to medium.
   Cover, and boil until the broth becomes rich and milky, 3 - 5 hours.
   Reduce the heat a little, as necessary, to maintain a moderate boil.
   Pour the milky broth through a colander into the pot or large bowl
   that contains the first batch.

9. Use the broth and shredded meat in another soup recipe, like Mandu Guk.
   Freeze any extra broth.
